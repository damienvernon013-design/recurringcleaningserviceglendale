Cleaning Considerations for Shared Community and Common Spaces

Glendale Recurring Cleaning | 22 years in the market

Shared common areas — lobbies, break rooms, shared restrooms, elevators, and multi-tenant corridors — present a different cleaning challenge than individual offices or workstations. No single person or department is responsible for them, which often means they get less consistent attention unless a cleaning plan explicitly assigns responsibility.

Why Common Areas Get Overlooked

In a multi-tenant building or a facility with shared amenities, common spaces sit in a gray zone. Individual tenants or departments maintain their own areas, but nobody owns the lobby, the shared kitchen, or the corridor — unless the building's cleaning contract specifically covers them.

What a Common-Area Scope Should Cover

  • Lobby and reception flooring, glass, and seating surfaces
  • Shared break rooms — countertops, tables, appliance exteriors
  • Shared restrooms — higher-frequency restocking and sanitization given multiple-tenant usage
  • Elevator interiors and corridor touchpoints
  • Shared entry doors and vestibules

Setting Clear Ownership

In multi-tenant commercial buildings, the cleanest approach is a single provider responsible for all common areas under one written scope, rather than leaving coverage to informal arrangements between tenants. That avoids the common failure mode where everyone assumes someone else is handling the shared kitchen or the restroom restocking.

First Impressions Ride on Common Areas

For multi-tenant buildings, the lobby and shared spaces are often the first thing a visitor sees — well before they reach any individual tenant's office. Our weekly and bi-weekly cleaning plans can be scoped specifically around shared-space responsibility for property managers and building owners.
