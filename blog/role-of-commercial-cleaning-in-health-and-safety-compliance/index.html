The Role of Commercial Cleaning in Workplace Health & Safety Compliance

Glendale Recurring Cleaning | 22 years in the market

Cleaning isn't just about appearance — it's tied directly to workplace safety compliance requirements that apply to nearly every commercial facility.

OSHA and Housekeeping Standards

OSHA General Industry Standards (29 CFR 1910.22) require that workplaces be kept clean, orderly, and sanitary; that floors be clean and dry; that aisles and passageways stay clear; and that drainage be maintained in wet areas.

Industry-Specific Requirements

Different industries layer additional standards on top of general housekeeping rules — healthcare facilities follow CDC guidelines, state health board rules, and accreditation standards, while other regulated industries have their own product and cleaning-frequency requirements set by state or local authorities.

Documentation as Protection

Cleaning logs (date, tasks, signatures), product safety data sheets (SDS), and written industry-specific protocols all serve as documentation if compliance is ever questioned.

Risk Management

Beyond compliance, clean workplaces reduce slip-and-fall incidents, chemical exposure events, and the spread of illness — all of which carry their own liability and cost implications.
