Pausing or Cancelling a Recurring Clean

What the contract terms actually say about pausing service, giving notice, and cancellation — and what to expect from the process.

Glendale Recurring Cleaning | 22 years in the market

Recurring cleaning contracts are long-term relationships, and they do not always run to completion without interruption. Building renovations, business relocations, tenant changes, and seasonal closures can all create situations where you need to pause or end a contract early. Here is how the process works and what to look for in the contract.

Pausing service

Most commercial cleaning contracts do not have a formal pause mechanism — they are either active or cancelled. If you need to stop service temporarily (for a renovation, a seasonal closure, or a facility shutdown), your options are typically: cancellation with re-engagement after the interruption, or negotiating a temporary suspension with the contractor.

Temporary suspensions are not always available and are not always in the contract. If you anticipate needing a pause in the future — for example, an office that closes for two weeks each December — negotiate the pause terms before signing. "Seasonal suspension with X days notice" is a legitimate contract term and any reputable contractor can accommodate it in writing.

What you should not do is stop scheduling visits without formally notifying the contractor. Monthly contracts may continue billing during an informal pause, and you may be liable for services that were scheduled but not performed.

Cancellation with proper notice

The standard cancellation process in the Glendale commercial cleaning market requires written notice — typically 30 days. "Written" in most contracts means email or physical letter to the address in the contract; a phone call is not written notice. Send the cancellation notice to the email or address specified in the contract and retain a copy with the date and time it was sent.

The 30-day notice period means that service continues — and billing continues — for 30 days after the notice is received. Do not stop paying during the notice period; that creates a collections situation where none is necessary.

Auto-renewal clauses

Many commercial cleaning contracts include auto-renewal provisions: if you do not give notice before a specified deadline (often 60 or 90 days before the end of the contract term), the contract renews automatically for another term. Read this section carefully when you sign, put a calendar reminder, and give the notice before the deadline even if you are happy with the service — you can always withdraw a cancellation notice, but you cannot undo an automatic renewal that has already triggered.

Early cancellation

Cancelling before the end of a contract term, without the notice period, typically triggers an early termination fee. The fee varies by contract but is commonly one to three months of the monthly rate. If your facility circumstances change materially — a building sale, a major tenant departure, a business closure — there is often room to negotiate an early termination without full fee liability, particularly if you give the contractor adequate advance notice. The contract governs, but contractors generally prefer a negotiated exit to a disputed one.
